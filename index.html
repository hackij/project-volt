Our customer wants a lighter car

From: Dr. Lena Vogt, Head of R&D
To: Materials Team
Subject: URGENT: customer request VOLT e1

Hi team,

our biggest customer, the car-sharing company CityFleet, wants to order 2,000 VOLT e1. But there is one condition in their contract: the car must not weigh more than 2,050 kg.

At the moment the VOLT e1 weighs 2,150 kg. The battery pack alone is about 500 kg, and we can't make it smaller. So we have to save at least 100 kg in the rest of the car, without making it less safe or much more expensive.

If we can't do this, CityFleet will buy from our competitor. Before you suggest new materials, I need you to know our materials and describe them precisely. Please send me your recommendations by Friday.

Discuss

Tricky cases

Titanium

Very strong – but with a density of 4.5 g/cm³ it is still a light metal. Used where strength and low weight matter.

Glass

Made from sand (a natural raw material) – but sand, soda and lime are melted together by people. So glass is an artificial material.

Carbon fibre

Carbon fibres alone are not enough: they are embedded in a plastic resin. Two materials combined = composite (CFRP).

    Language toolkit

    How to describe and compare materials

    Classify
    Aluminium is a light metal. · CFRP belongs to the group of composites.
    Describe
    Copper is (very / extremely / relatively) ductile. · It has a high / low density / strength / conductivity.
    Explain the use
    Copper is used for the motor windings because it conducts electricity very well. · Glass is suitable for windows because it is hard and transparent.
    Compare
    Aluminium is lighter than steel. · CFRP is much stiffer but more expensive than aluminium.
    Decide
    We have decided to replace steel with aluminium for the bonnet. This saves about … kg.
    Reject & trade-offs
    We decided against carbon-ceramic brakes because they are too expensive. · However, magnesium needs corrosion protection.
    Notes: Kurz gemeinsam lesen, 2–3 Beispielsätze mündlich bilden lassen. Diese Redemittel brauchen die Schüler:innen direkt für den Decision Report.
